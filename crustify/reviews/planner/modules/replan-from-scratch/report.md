## Foundational modules against the raw-lifetime tiers

The tiers held one synthetic `raw-lifetime` unit each (waves 0 and 2), with no files: their binders discovered the `void` and `string` routines themselves. The current specs select foundational TUs from the graph, read each candidate's body, and record each member's role in the ownership store:

- `src/util/alloc.c` [2]: `git__malloc`, `git__calloc`, `git__mallocarray` (alloc constructors), `git__free` (dropper), `git__strdup`, `git__strndup`, `git__substrdup` (deep cloners of a string), the `git__allocator` vtable and `git_allocator`; `git__realloc` and `git__reallocarray` take no role (realloc-style).
- `src/util/allocators/failalloc.c` [1], `stdalloc.c` [4], `debugalloc.c` [3], `win32_leakcheck.c` [5]: the allocator implementations behind `git_allocator`.
- `src/util/strlist.c` [6]: string arrays (`git_strlist_copy*` cloners, `git_strlist_free*` droppers).
- `deps/reftable/basics.c` [0]: reftable's own allocator family behind `reftable_set_alloc` (`reftable_malloc`, `reftable_calloc`, `reftable_realloc`, `reftable_free`, `reftable_strdup`), `free_names`/`parse_names` and `reftable_buf` (init constructor, disposer).

None needs a foundational split: every item of these TUs depends on foundational modules only, but for `git_error_set_oom`/`git_error_set` on failure paths, which the order cuts. Only `alloc.c` and `stdalloc.c` get waves (54 and 56, each with its review wave): the public API reaches the allocator family through the owned `char *` and `void *` fields of public structs (`git_signature.name`, ...), and `git_stdalloc_init_allocator` is declared in `include/git2/sys/alloc.h`; nothing public reaches `reftable` or `strlist`, so they are scheduled lazily, when a consumer appears.
